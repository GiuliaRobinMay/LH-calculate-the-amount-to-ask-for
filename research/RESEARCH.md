### 2. The expert rules (quoted)

1. Itemize from real quotes — NCOA: "Costs should be based on actual quotes and/or estimates to
   ensure that they aren't padded or underestimated." Grantboost: a round "$10,000 for supplies"
   reads as a guess.
2. Never lowball, never pad — TGCI Goldilocks rule: "If it's too large or too small, it won't
   do… an unrealistic budget in either direction will kill the credibility of your proposal."
   Grantsights: funders "universally reject" miscellaneous/contingency lines.
3. Last money in / duplication of benefits — Salvation Army: "if you owe $1,000 and they can give
   $300, they will not give you a check until you prove you have paid the other $700." FEMA
   "cannot pay for that same loss again"; order is insurance → FEMA → SBA.
4. One-time money does not cover ongoing costs — ACF: TANF non-recurrent benefits "will not
   extend beyond four months" and are "not intended to meet recurrent or ongoing needs."
5. Stay in the funder's range — GrantWatch: a $25K–$50K funder will not "suddenly award
   $200,000." Candid: a $25,000 ask from a $5,000-top funder "raises a red flag."
6. First-timers get less; most doors open once per 12 months — Candid; Instrumentl example:
   median new-grantee award $15,000 vs repeat over $150,000.
7. Count related costs — FEMA rental aid covers "security deposit, monthly rent, and
   utilities"; business grants are taxable income (USU Extension).
8. Never ask for 100% of a project — Candid; 2 CFR 200.306 on cost sharing and in-kind.

Candid's percentage matrix: high alignment + high capacity + existing relationship → 70–90% of
the project budget; same but no relationship → 40–60%; moderate alignment → 20–40%; moderate /
moderate → 10–20%; renewals +5–10%.

### 4. First-timer mistakes (documented)

Asking outside the funder's range; round numbers without backup; no quotes; budget and
narrative that disagree; ongoing costs from one-time funds; forgetting that charities pay the
gap; forgetting deposits, fees and taxes; stacking sources after a disaster; asking too little
from a once-a-year door.

### 5. Data on ask size vs. success

Partial funding is normal (community foundations "partially fund to spread the wealth").
Research-funding studies (Denmark, Norway) show success rates falling as average grant size
rises. GrantStation 2024: 88% of applicants received at least one award. Chicago Homelessness
Prevention Call Center: one-time payments of up to $1,500 cut shelter entry by 76% (Science,
2016). A vendor claim that "40% of failed applications have budget problems" has no primary
source — do not use it.

## Proposed question set

See the brief. Path one (a cost I already have): what it costs and where each number comes
from; one-time vs monthly with months counted; what is already covered; related costs; have
you asked here before and do you know their limit. Path two (business / nonprofit / project):
line items with a reason each; own share and in-kind; the funder's cap and habit; first time.
Output: the gap as the ask, a one-sentence version to say, a printable budget table, flags for
guesses, over-cap asks, ongoing costs and asks bigger than one door, and one community link
per line.
